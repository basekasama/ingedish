## What is Ingedish?

**Ingedish** is a language game based on English words and morphemes.

It lets you attach familiar English prefixes and suffixes more freely than standard English normally allows.

For example:

```text
why
→ whying

because
→ becausing

between
→ betweened

everything
→ everythinging

love
→ loving
→ lovinged
→ lovingedish
```

These forms do not need to be grammatically valid English.

They only need to be recoverable enough that someone can look at the pieces, the order, the syntax, and the context and think:

> “I think I know what that means.”

That is the central idea of Ingedish.

> **Understandability > grammaticality.**

Or more simply:

> **If you understood it, it worked.**

Ingedish is a language game, not a proposal to replace English and not currently a complete constructed language.

It is English morphology being stretched, bent, stacked, and occasionally broken while meaning is still hanging on.

## ING・ED・ISH

The name itself is:

```text
ING + ED + ISH
= INGEDISH
```

while also looking suspiciously like someone misspelled *English*.

`-ing`, `-ed`, and `-ish` are especially useful in Ingedish, but they do not have strict formal definitions here.

They broadly inherit the intuitions they already have in English.

### `-ing`

Roughly gives something an **-ing / present-participle-like** quality.

```text
why
→ whying

with
→ withing

everything
→ everythinging
```

`whying` might mean asking, thinking, or doing *why*.

`withing` might mean being *with* someone.

`everythinging` might turn *everything* into an action, process, or state.

### `-ed`

Roughly gives something an **-ed / past / completed / resulting-state-like** quality.

```text
between
→ betweened

nothing
→ nothinged

loving
→ lovinged
```

Depending on context, it may suggest that something happened, became established, or ended up in some resulting state.

`-ed` does **not** simply mean “passive”.

### `-ish`

Roughly gives something an **adjectival, approximate, weakened, or “sort of”** quality.

```text
lovinged
→ lovingedish
```

Something `lovingedish` might be:

```text
kind of lovinged
sort of lovinged
lovinged-like
seemingly lovinged
```

The exact interpretation is deliberately left to context.

## Affixes behave like operators

A useful way to think about Ingedish is that every new affix can act on the meaning produced so far.

```text
love
→ loving
→ lovinged
→ lovingedish
```

Conceptually, this can be imagined as:

```text
ISH(ED(ING(love)))
```

The notation is only a way of thinking.

Ingedish is not intended to be a formal typed semantic system.

The important part is simply that **order can matter**.

---

## Words are suffixable

In standard English, the grammatical category of a word strongly restricts what can be attached to it.

In Ingedish, those restrictions become suggestions.

```text
why → whying
because → becausing
if → ifing
what → whating
with → withing
everything → everythinging
```

A preposition can become action-like.

A question word can become process-like.

A noun can become predicate-like.

The result may be strange.

Strange is allowed.

---

## Parts of speech are suggestions

An Ingedished word does not necessarily have one fixed part of speech.

Compare:

> **I am whying you.**

> **My whying never stops.**

> **That’s a very whying cat.**

The same `whying` can work in different syntactic roles while still carrying a recognizable relationship to *why*.

Likewise:

> **I everythinging you.**

and:

> **This is my everythinging.**

use the same form differently.

A useful description is:

> **Morphology builds meaning. Syntax gives it a job.**

---

## Prefixes and scope

Prefixes such as:

```text
un-
re-
over-
under-
```

may also be used.

For example:

```text
unlovinged
```

may naturally be read as:

```text
un + (love + ing + ed)
```

but another grouping may also be possible if the context supports it.

If the grouping matters, hyphens can clarify it:

```text
un-lovinged
unlove-ing-ed
re-un-I-ed
```

Hyphens are optional.

> **Ambiguity is playable.**

---

## Spelling is flexible

Ingedish does not require one spelling convention.

For example:

```text
hope + ing
```

might become:

```text
hoping
hopeing
hope-ing
```

Standard English spelling may be used for readability.

Morpheme boundaries may also be preserved when that makes the construction easier to see.

A useful principle is:

> **Spelling is flexible. Morphemes should remain recoverable.**

The goal is not random misspelling.

The reader should still be able to see what happened.

> “You spelled it wrong.”

> **“No. I Ingedished it.”**

---

## Existing English is still Ingedishable

Already being an English word does not protect something from further Ingedishing.

```text
everything
→ everythinging
```

There is no requirement to consult etymology or find the historically correct morpheme boundary first.

If a useful structure can be perceived, it can be played with.

---

## Comprehensibility

Ingedish is not simply divided into:

```text
correct
incorrect
```

Some expressions are immediately understandable.

Some are ambiguous.

Some are difficult but recoverable.

Some collapse into complete nonsense.

For example:

> **My whying never stops.**

is relatively easy to interpret.

Meanwhile:

> **I sleepingedish you.**

requires considerably more work, but a reader may still construct a meaning from it.

As morphology gets deeper, interpretation usually becomes harder.

There is no formal threshold.

Ingedish is free, but not arbitrary.

The pieces should still give the reader something to decode.

---

## `-ness`

Because syntax can already make an Ingedished form noun-like, `-ness` is not required just to create a noun.

> **My whying never stops.**

already treats `whying` as an activity or concept.

But `-ness` can make that conceptualization more explicit:

```text
lovingedish
→ lovingedishness
```

It may describe the state, quality, or condition of being `lovingedish`.

---

## Repetition

Affixes may also be repeated:

```text
lovingedish
lovingedishish
lovingedishishish
```

Ingedish does not define a formal meaning for the number of repetitions.

Repetition may express approximation, emphasis, hesitation, playfulness, or whatever the context makes understandable.

Like:

```text
lol
loool
looooool
```

Some things do not need to be grammarized.

## Some things are better left unwhyinged.

One representative Ingedish sentence is:

> **Some things are better left unwhyinged.**

Its approximate meaning might be:

> Some things are better left without asking why.

Part of what makes the sentence understandable is the familiar English scaffolding:

> *Some things are better left ...*

English syntax helps the reader decode unfamiliar morphology.

That interaction is a major part of Ingedish.

---

## I everythinging you.

Another representative expression is:

> **I everythinging you.**

What exactly does `everythinging` mean?

Love?

Want?

Give everything to?

Do everything for?

Make someone your everything?

Perhaps all of those.

Perhaps none of them exactly.

The ambiguity is part of the expression.

The reader does not always need to recover one dictionary definition.

Sometimes:

> **If you understood it, it worked.**

is enough.

## Ingedish poetry

> After I happenedinged to look at you,  
> I became lovingedish of you.
>
> There was no meaning in  
> ifing, whying, becausing,  
> or even whating.
>
> I started un-I-ing for you.
>
> **I everythinging you.**

This is not a grammatical model that other Ingedish must follow.

It is simply one example of what Ingedish can be used for.

## Open Question: Tense and Aspect

Tense and aspect are intentionally left open in **Ingedish v0.1**.

For example, should past tense appear inside the Ingedished form?

```text
happeningeded
```

Or should ordinary English syntax carry tense around it?

```text
is happeninged
was happeninged
has been happeninged
might be happeninged
```

Both approaches create interesting interpretations and problems of their own.

Rather than defining a rule prematurely:

> **Tense and aspect remain experimental.**

Some things are better left unwhyinged.

---

## What Ingedish is not

Ingedish is not a proposal to replace English.

It is not currently a fully specified constructed language.

And it is not simply random broken English.

Its interest lies in how far familiar pieces can be stretched while meaning remains recoverable.

English gives the reader expectations.

Ingedish breaks some of them.

The reader tries to decode what remains.

Sometimes it works immediately.

Sometimes it takes effort.

Sometimes it fails.

All three are interesting.
